# Topic Discovery

## Purpose
Help a speaker who can answer questions but struggles to initiate a talk generate **three viable speaking topics**.

## When to use
Before preparing a camera explanation, a meeting contribution, or an informal public talk. Use when the input is a broad knowledge area or “I don't know what to say.”

## Inputs
- Audience and occasion (if known)
- Recent real observations, decisions, mistakes, surprises, or questions
- Any limits on what may be shared publicly

If no experiences are supplied, ask **one focused question** about a recent surprise, disagreement, lesson, or decision. Do not manufacture a biography.

## Procedure
1. Capture raw material in the speaker's own words.
2. Search for a change of mind, useful distinction, practical lesson, unresolved question, or common error.
3. Generate three candidate topics, each as a **question a listener might care about**, not only a category.
4. For each candidate, state audience relevance and the evidence the speaker actually has.
5. Mark assumptions and missing evidence. Do not assert an unsupported hot take.
6. Invite the speaker to select one candidate. The speaker owns the editorial judgment.

## Output
For each of three candidates:
- **Topic/question**
- **Why listeners might care**
- **Source observation**
- **What evidence is available / missing**
- **Possible angle** (tentative, not attributed to the speaker)

## Quality checklist
Specific, audience-relevant, grounded in real material, feasible in the available speaking time, and distinct from the other candidates.

## Failure modes
Generic trends, fictitious anecdotes, forced controversy, premature drafting of a polished script.

## Practice
Once per day, note one surprising observation. Generate three questions from it; select one without writing a full script. Continue with [Point Extraction](../point-extraction/SKILL.md).
