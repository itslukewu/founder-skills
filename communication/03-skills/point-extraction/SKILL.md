# Point Extraction

## Purpose
Turn a broad topic or unordered thoughts into **one sentence the speaker genuinely wants to communicate**.

## Inputs
The chosen topic, raw thoughts or transcript, intended audience, and desired takeaway. Distinguish observations from interpretations.

## Procedure
1. Repeat the speaker's ideas neutrally in short phrases.
2. Identify candidate claims (what is being asserted) rather than categories (what is being discussed).
3. Offer up to three distinct candidate claims, distinguishing factual, interpretive, and prescriptive statements.
4. Ask the speaker to select, correct, or reject a claim. Do not silently assign an AI-origin opinion to them.
5. Check: Is it specific? Is it contestable or meaningful? Can it be supported? Is the scope appropriately qualified?
6. Link the approved claim to one or two reasons and one real supporting example. Clearly flag missing support.
7. Produce a short speaking card; do **not** default to a full written speech.

## Output
- **Approved core claim:** one sentence (or “not yet approved”)
- **Why it matters to the audience:** one sentence
- **Support:** up to two reasons and one example
- **Uncertainty:** what should be verified or qualified
- **Keywords:** three to five cues for speaking

## Example
Topic: “AI coding”
Tentative point: “Defining who may change product decisions is as important as writing the implementation prompt.”
Evidence needed: a documented process or example, not a made-up incident.

## Failure modes
Vague truisms, multiple claims fused together, AI-generated convictions, overstated certainty, stories with fabricated specifics.

## Practice
Take five disconnected notes, reduce them to one claim, then speak 30 seconds without reading prose. Use the [PREP guide](../../02-frameworks/prep.md) if helpful.
