# PREP — Point, Reason, Example, Point

## Purpose

Deliver one understandable, supported point in a short spoken contribution (roughly 30–90 seconds). PREP is a helpful scaffold, not a mandatory script or universal formula.

## Structure

1. **Point:** State what you think in one sentence.
2. **Reason:** Explain why in plain language.
3. **Example:** Give a real incident, comparison, observation, or appropriately qualified evidence.
4. **Point:** Restate the implication or next action, without merely repeating your first sentence.

## Example

**Point:** “An AI coding project needs a clear product specification, not only better prompts.”

**Reason:** “Prompts alone may not define who has authority to change user-visible product decisions.”

**Example:** “In a project, accepting unreviewed changes to permissions could produce an implementation that differs from the founder’s intention.” *(Hypothetical scenario, not a claim about a particular project.)*

**Point:** “Set decision boundaries and acceptance criteria before delegating implementation.”

## 60-second rehearsal

- 10 seconds: Point
- 15 seconds: Reason
- 25 seconds: Example
- 10 seconds: So what / Point

Timing is indicative; prioritize natural speech over mechanical proportions.

## When not to use

Use a question-first opening when the audience needs the problem before the answer, or a longer analytical structure when comparing multiple options. A personal story may need chronology and tension instead.

## Checklist

- Is the Point a claim rather than a topic?
- Does the Reason directly support it?
- Is the Example factual or clearly labeled hypothetical?
- Does the ending add an implication?
- Can a listener repeat the central message after one hearing?
