# Active vs. Reactive Speaking

## The distinction

**Reactive speaking** starts with an external prompt: a question defines the topic and boundaries. **Active speaking** requires the speaker to select a topic, decide what matters to an audience, form a claim, and explain it without being asked.

Someone may give excellent answers yet struggle to start a camera monologue. This is a useful **working hypothesis**, not a psychological diagnosis or universal learner type.

## The tasks hidden in “just talk about it”

- **Topic selection:** Which observation or experience is worth sharing?
- **Point selection:** What single claim will the listener take away?
- **Support:** What makes the claim reasonable?
- **Sequencing:** What must the listener know first?
- **Delivery:** Can the speaker retrieve the structure in real time?

Difficulty in any one task can feel like “I know a lot but have nothing to say.”

## Practical intervention

Instead of starting with a broad noun like “AI agents,” begin with a complete sentence:

> “I used to think X, but after observing Y, I now think Z.”

Other prompts: “A decision I changed my mind about…,” “A mistake I noticed…,” “An assumption I would challenge…,” “A question my audience keeps getting wrong…”.

Do not require a contrarian view; an accurate explanation is valuable even without disagreement.

## Self-test

Record two 60-second clips on a topic you know:
1. Answer a specific question given by someone else.
2. Independently choose the angle and explain a point.

Compare time to first point, coherence, evidence, and audience relevance using the [rubric](../05-practice/speaking-rubric.md). The result is a personal baseline, not proof of a fixed trait.

## Failure modes

- Mistaking topic labels for claims (“talk about coding”).
- Including every associated idea rather than selecting one.
- Using an AI-generated opinion that is not actually your own.
- Solving delivery anxiety with structure alone; pressure and performance also require practice.
